## 0. Recommendation on one screen

1. **Oracle-first, plan 06-01 contains no C and records the STIC page-reading verdict before any implementation plan starts** (V6-1). My own pre-read of the cached catalogs says the verdict will almost surely be "the tier-1 STIC pages print structure and the sync handshake, not the register map, the BACKTAB bit layout or the Color Stack" [OBS, section 1]. So the plan must be written for that case, not hope for the other.
2. **Two layers, never mixed** (V6-3). Structure the sources do print (20x12 cards, 19 columns of 8 pixels plus a 7-pixel 20th column, 159 x 96 visible, 240 background words of 14 bits, 16 colours, 13 or 14 row requests per frame) is checked as closed-form property tests. Policy numbers (window lengths, hold lengths, interrupt width, enable sustain, power-on state) are `unknown-default-chosen`: tests prove the machine implements the policy through profile perturbation and accounting identities, and a policy number is never the expected value of its own test.
3. **Hand-derived expected frames are literal text** (V6-4, V6-5): about 8 frames, one hex digit per pixel, written before the core renders anything, with no second renderer anywhere. The gate guards the hand-derivation with arithmetic that is not a renderer (grammar, size, per-card pixel-count conservation against the card tables, a hash tie to the `.expect` file). A blind second reader derives the same frames independently from the card tables and rule notes; the diff is the highest-value cross-check available.
4. **Non-circular properties carry the weight where tables cannot**: frame-chain and frame-bytes slicing invariance, time tiling and a bus-steal accounting identity, frame-event counting that is not a modulo, side-effect-free inspection as byte equality of the whole instance plus a positive control pair (the CPU read of `$0021` DOES switch the mode, the inspect read does not), the display-enable handshake as sequence tables, metamorphic relations between the two card modes (V6-6, V6-7, V6-8).
5. **One new original diagnostic `ivs-diag-stic-1`** (about 130 words, one SDBD-bearing listing, no interrupt, boot-stub contract 1 unchanged), run in two scenarios that differ only by a controller input at tick 0 selecting FG/BG or Color Stack, with 6 program mutants and an installed-consumer replay (V6-9). The stub is NOT changed in Phase 6, so every Phase 4 and 5 golden keeps its CPU, memory and image digests byte for byte, and that identity is itself the regression proof that a display-off STIC is timing-neutral (V6-19).
6. **D4 is not crossed by Phase 6** (three diagnostics is not above three; labels and macros are not needed), but the SDBD rule the listing checker lacks becomes necessary, and a pre-committed crossing rule plus effort data feed the mini-assembler decision that stays at the start of Phase 7 (V6-10).
7. **Budget: do a small lever pass at the phase start and change the metric** (V6-12). Both hosted triggers already fire (last-5 medians: gate 106 s against 90, cpu 336 against 280 [OBS]) while the speed-normalised cost `cpu_s / build_s` is a stable 6.4 to 7.4 since Phase 5 [OBS, computed]. Plan 06-02 applies the pre-committed `-g0` on fault targets, records the normalised metric, and asks the orchestrator to re-baseline on it. Phase 6 caps: at most 28 new named faults, at most 25 controls and at most one control that reaches the C symbol layer (a control of that class costs about 2.2 hosted cpu-s, ten times a text control [OBS 05-L1]).
8. **Realistic sizing**: Phase 5's estimate was +17 to +28 hosted cpu-s and the realised growth was about +55 (2 to 2.6 times); the same factor applied to this phase's bottom-up figure of about +24 gives +48 to +62, which would end above the cpu trigger even at median runner speed (V6-11). The plan list therefore carries cost checkpoints with a stop rule.
9. **Handed to the owner, non-blocking**: the subjective look of a frame (an operator-side PPM export, NOT evidence), D10 measurements (9 new `D10-VID` items plus one D11 item, on top of the 12 system items already listed), D11 real-EXEC video, and a tier-1 or tier-2 register-level STIC document if the owner holds one (V6-20).
10. **Eleven plans in eleven sequential waves plus one conditional lever** (V6-23), oracle plan first, five-task caps, `sh`, `dash` and the container proxy `sh tools/gate-linux.sh` on every plan that adds C.

## 1. Facts that shape the strategy [OBS]

**What the tier-1 STIC pages print (my pre-read of the cached catalogs).**
- GI 1980 printed 5-10 to 5-12 (pdf 191 to 193) and GI 1982 printed 9-43 to 9-45 are the AY-3-8900 and AY-3-8900-1 pages. Page 1 is features, pin list and description, page 2 is the electrical characteristics table, page 3 is three timing diagrams. Printed 5-8, 5-9 / 9-41, 9-42 are the 8900 system overview.
- Printed text, quoted by content: eight foreground objects on a 168H by 104V grid (8900) or 167H by 105V (8900-1), "of which 159 x 96 are visible positions"; a background of 20H x 12V cards of 8 x 8 picture elements, "19 are composed of 8 x 8 picture elements and the 20th 7 x 8"; a dedicated external memory of 240 by 14 bit words for the card control codes; an 8-bit-wide graphics pattern memory; 16 selectable colours; clock 4.000 MHz (8900) or 3.579545 MHz (8900-1); a multiplexed 14-bit bus.
- Printed synchronisation text: SR1 occurs once per frame and "indicates that STIC time is complete and that the AY-3-8900-1 has switched to the CPU controlled mode"; SR2 "is issued thirteen or fourteen times per picture frame depending on picture offset" and requests the first line of a new row of twenty characters; the first line of a card is fetched by SR2 together with SR3, "the following fifteen lines to complete the 8 x 8 array are fetched by SR3 alone"; SR3 pulses once per character position; BC1, BC2, BDIR are "only validated if the STIC is in the CPU controlled mode".
- NOT printed in the text layer of any of those six pages (a search of `register`, `BACKTAB`, `color stack`, `display enable`, `card` finds none on them): a register address map, a display-enable strobe, the BACKTAB bit layout (only its width: 14 bits), the card-number and colour fields, the Color Stack, a foreground/background mode switch, colour numbering, any VBLANK access-window length, any bus-request hold length. The 1982 catalog adds nothing beyond the 1980 text (same three pages, same words). `refs/` holds the two catalogs (hash-registered, gitignored).
- [INF] Therefore the tier-1 evidence for VID-01..04 is structure (geometry, counts, the SR1/SR2/SR3 handshake, the existence of a CPU-controlled mode that starts at SR1). Every register, field and window number is tier 4 (one community lineage), tier 2 (the 2609 overview page, read in earlier phases as a read-only reference) or a labelled default. This mirrors Phase 5's PSG verdict (`equations-printed: no`) and the controller table (tier-4 LOW, one lineage).
- [INF] Cross-checks the pages DO allow: 12 rows x 16 TV lines per card = 192 active lines, which equals the 192 lines of the community and GIMINI figures; "13 or 14" row requests per frame bounds the number of bus-steal events per frame independently of any emulator; 159 + 1 = the 160-column nominal grid of C10.
- [INF, arithmetic from the profile] The profile's 262 lines x 228 STIC clocks = 59,736 ticks is 59.9227 Hz; an NTSC frame is 262.5 lines x 227.5 colour-burst clocks = 59,718.75 ticks (59.94 Hz). The profile frame is 0.03 percent longer than a broadcast frame (C7 and C9 territory). Worth one discrepancy line, not a test.

The priority at an interruptible boundary is stop, BUSRQ, INTR, INTRM (POL-INT-01).

They are asserted by `psg.bounds.g-and-u` over every decle and by `IvsPsgTable.cmake`, which refuses a profile whose published figures disagree with the formula (05-08 summary).
- [INF] A bus-steal modelled as one-cycle hold units leaves U unchanged and can only lengthen the gap g; this is what to re-prove, not what to assume (V6-18).
- The CPU cannot see a frame: frames are timeline events (D-09, `docs/time-base.md`: "never a modulo"). A halted CPU stops a run before any unit begins. `ivs_machine_peek` is side-effect-free by contract and `assert mem` uses it.
- Listing checker `tools/check/listing.cmake` models branch targets (since 05-01) but "an SDBD prefix is not modelled: such a listing fails closed" (header comment, line 20). A diagnostic that writes 14-bit BACKTAB words needs SDBD or shift tricks (V6-9).
- Stub contract 1 (`docs/diagnostics.md`): the interrupt entry at `1004` is an unexpected-interrupt trap that halts; the stub provides no routine, vector or service to a cartridge; the gate enforces at most 16 non-zero words. Changing a word is a new contract and changes `digest.images` in every golden.

## 2. Oracle taxonomy: what is independent, what is circular [REC]

The same author writes the literals, the derivations, the listing and the core, and the second reader is the same model family. "Independent" below means independent of the emulator's execution and of its tables, not of the author (as in `docs/diagnostics.md`).

| Oracle kind | Independent of the core? | Used for | Tier it can support |
|---|---|---|---|
| GI printed structure: 20 x 12 cards, 19 x 8 + 7 px, 159 x 96 visible, 240 x 14-bit words, 16 colours, graphics memory 8 bits wide, 13 or 14 SR2 per frame, 16 TV lines per card, 12 x 16 = 192 lines | yes (page text; the image of 5-12 still to read) | VID-03 geometry literal, VID-02 steal-count bound, VID-04 structure | `source-expectation` (tier 1 for structure) |
| Tier-4 and tier-2 rule notes (register map, BACKTAB fields, card pattern bit order, Color Stack advance and wrap, mode switch) transcribed in plan 06-01 and read blind by a second reader | partly (second read of one lineage) | VID-01 register literal, VID-04 rules | `source-expectation, second-read`, tier-4 LOW, `provisional` |
| Hand-derived expected frames (`data/video/frames/*.frame`), written from card tables and rule notes before the core renders, plus a second reader's independent derivation of the same frames | yes (no code involved); shared-misreading risk is real | VID-04, VID-03 end-to-end, SC4 | `source-expectation` for the frame; `original-diagnostic` for the guest-produced frame |
| Per-card pixel-count conservation: for every 8 x 8 block of a frame literal, the number of foreground-coloured pixels equals the popcount of the card pattern literal | yes (arithmetic, not a renderer) | catches hand-derivation typos in 96 lines of 159 digits | guard (no tier) |
| Closed-form counting properties: frames completed in `t` ticks equals the count of events in the timeline, not `floor(t / F)` by modulo (events are placed by the timeline); SR2-class bus-steal events per frame lie in {13, 14} when the display is enabled and are 0 when disabled | yes (event-count arithmetic; the bound is printed) | VID-02, VID-03 | `source-expectation` (the bound), `deterministic-repeatability` (the counts) |
| Time tiling and the steal accounting identity: the sum of unit elapsed cycles equals elapsed cycles exactly; cycles the CPU executed plus cycles in grant units equals the total; a constant-cost loop guest's iteration count equals floor((total - stolen) / loop cost) with the loop cost from the CP-1610 cycle table | yes (accounting, cycle table is tier 1) | VID-02 | `deterministic-repeatability` with a tier-1 cycle table |
| Slicing invariance: the frame bytes, the video chain, the frame count and the instance bytes are equal for one call, fixed, prime, seeded and one-unit slicings | yes (no table involved) | VID-03, VID-02, API-03 extension | `deterministic-repeatability` |
| Side-effect-free inspection: `memcmp` of the whole instance storage before and after every inspect call, over every STIC address in every window state, with a positive control that the CPU read of `$0021` changes the instance in the same state | yes (byte equality; the pair prevents a vacuous pass) | VID-06 | `deterministic-repeatability`; structural |
| Display-enable handshake sequences: tables of (write, frame boundary, write ...) giving frame produced or not | partly (the sequence rule is tier 4 and a default, C6; the table is hand-written) | VID-01 | `source-expectation` (tier 4), policy rows labelled |
| Metamorphic relations between the card modes and positions (a card at another position gives the same 8 x 8 block shifted by whole cards; FG/BG and Color Stack agree when the colour inputs are made equal by construction; permuting BACKTAB words permutes card blocks) | yes (relations between two runs of the same core; they catch asymmetric bugs, not shared misreadings) | VID-04 | guard (repeatability) |
| Profile perturbation: change one profile field through the descriptor seam, require the predicted output change and no other (`stic.profile.<field>-changes-output`) | yes (behavioural proof that a number is data, not a constant) | all policy numbers | none by itself |
| `cmake -E sha256sum` over the ASCII text of a frame literal | yes (not the core's SHA-256) | the tie between `.frame` file and `.expect` assert | `deterministic-repeatability` (framing only) |
| Self-recorded `.digests` goldens | **no** (repeat only) | regression pins | `deterministic-repeatability` |

**Circular, to avoid (anti-patterns):**
1. A reference renderer in a test, in cmake or in shell that expands BACKTAB and GRAM into pixels. It is a second STIC with the same author's misreading, as the second CPU interpreter and the cmake PSG model were rejected in Phases 4 and 5. The only arithmetic the gate does on a frame is the popcount conservation and the hash tie.
2. Generating a `.frame` file or a `.expect` assert from a run, or from the core's own render function; the derivation is committed before the program first renders (Phase 4 rule).
3. Expected values that restate a policy number: the window length, the hold length, the interrupt low width, the sustain rule. Such a test says "the core equals its own profile". Use perturbation (the number is data), accounting identities (the number cancels) and the printed bounds (13 or 14).
4. Geometry constants read from the profile or `ivs_info` on both sides of a test. The expected width, height and column count are literals in `data/video/geometry.literal.txt` written from the page, never derived from the field they judge (the memmap-literal independence rule).
5. Any frame, palette, register table, window number, colour-stack rule or bit order from jzIntv or MAME as an expected value or as a "second opinion" (tier 5, AGENTS.md; jzIntv is GPL-2.0-or-later and MAME is BSD-3-Clause per file). They may only appear as `unknown-default-chosen` hints in the discrepancy log, never copied, never agreement evidence.
6. Using the GROM image or a GROM glyph as the visual oracle (rights-bearing, revision-specific). Frames use GRAM cards the fixture defines, plus the zero GROM stand-in (which renders as background only, itself a test).
7. A 160th-column choice or a not-displayed marker decided by editing a golden. The geometry literal and the profile field move together in one reviewed change.
8. One plan writing the literal and the implementation in one commit (the memmap precedent). SUMMARYs name the oracle commit that precedes the implementation commit.

## 3. Decisions

### A. Evidence architecture

**V6-1 [REC] Oracle-first plan 06-01, no C, page-reading verdict recorded first.** The plan produces only data and documents: `docs/stic/reference-notes.md` (page-cited rules, the verdict keys below, a table of every rule with its source tier), `data/stic/rules.json` (rule inventory `STIC-R01..`, each with statement, `basis` in {`tier1-literal`, `tier1-derived`, `tier2-bsr`, `tier4-community`, `default-unknown`}, source locators, the profile fields it reads, covering vector ids, a discrepancy id; same shape as `data/psg/rules.json`), `data/stic/intv-2609-ntsc-family-p1.literal.txt` (register addresses, widths, read/write behaviour per address, tag, `src=`, `conf=`; grammar copied from the memmap literal), `data/video/geometry.literal.txt`, `data/video/cards.literal.txt` (card pattern bit order, BACKTAB field widths, Color Stack rules, each with tag and source), `data/video/frames/*.frame` and `docs/video/frame-derivations.md`, `docs/video/diag-stic-1-derivation.md` (the diagnostic's expected values with every step shown, before the listing exists), line-based vector files `data/stic/vectors/*.txt` (text, not JSON, so no `string(JSON)` re-parse), the discrepancy allocation table (one table for all new ids, Phase 5 lesson: next free id is C81), the claim allocation (next is CL-0016), the `d10-measurements.ids` extension, the gate step `video` with its controls, the SDBD rule of `listing.cmake` with controls, and the blind second-read requests. Implementation plans cite these files by path. The page-reading verdict is a block of keys at the top of `reference-notes.md` that the `video` step checks exists:
`registers-printed`, `backtab-format-printed`, `color-stack-printed`, `display-enable-printed`, `colors-numbered-printed`, `window-lengths-printed`, `bus-hold-printed`, `rows-per-frame-printed`, `visible-geometry-printed`, `timing-figure-readable` (each `yes`, `no`, or `partial` plus the page). My pre-read predicts `no` for the first seven, `yes` for the next two and `unknown` for the last until the image of printed 5-12 / 9-45 is read at page-image resolution (as 05-01 did with `pdftoppm` for the PSG pages). Decision tree: if any of the first five turns out `yes`, its rules become tier-1 vectors and the claim ceiling for that area rises to `source-expectation` with tier 1; otherwise the area is tier 4 / labelled default and stays `provisional` with D10 or a tier 1 to 3 document as the only upgrade. Phase 5 precedent: `equations-printed: no` was recorded and the vectors were re-tiered per rule, not dropped.

**V6-2 [REC] Source ladder, second reads, and what is read from where.** In plan 06-01: (1) GI 1980 5-10 to 5-12 and GI 1982 9-43 to 9-45 at page-image resolution, for the SR1/SR2/SR3 and CPU-time diagrams, which may print relative timings; (2) the 2609 overview page (`SRC-bsr-2609`, tier 2, hash-registered) for the display and card descriptions already used in earlier phases; (3) the community wiki memory-map and STIC pages (tier 4; `SRC-intv-wiki`, a recorded revision) for register and card semantics. Community emulator documentation (`SRC-community-emu-docs`) stays `reference-only`: used to find the questions and the discrepancies, never as a row's `src=` and never copied. Blind second reads (copy `docs/psg/second-reader-protocol.md`; reader sees only the page text or image, the protocol and the card tables it must derive from; no `data/`, `core/`, `.planning/`, other emulators; copies placed under `build/review/` by `tools/review`, the Phase 5 lesson that stops a reader seeing the answer): (a) one reader transcribes the register literal and the BACKTAB and card rule notes from the sources; (b) one reader derives every expected frame from only the card tables, the rule notes and the BACKTAB/GRAM contents (the hand-derived frames are the thing most worth a second derivation: 8 frames, 96 lines each, one typo is enough to make a golden wrong); (c) one reader transcribes the printed structure into `geometry.literal.txt`. Records are `source-expectation, second-read`, never `independent-implementation-comparison`. A disagreement is resolved from the source, written into the literal's note, and counted (`blind-files=`, `second-read=` tokens already exist). Rights: page images and text stay in the gitignored cache; only derived tables and short paraphrases are committed (`data/sources.json` entries `rights: unknown`, `capture: hashed`).

**V6-3 [REC] Two layers, tested separately, with one rule between them.** Layer S (structure): what a source prints (geometry, counts, the CPU-controlled mode starting at SR1, 13 or 14 requests per frame, 16 TV lines per card row). Layer P (policy): everything else. The rule: *a policy number is never the expected value of its own test.* Layer S tests state the printed fact as a literal. Layer P tests prove one of three things: the number is profile data (perturbation), the core's behaviour is consistent with the number across time (accounting identity, tiling), or the tier tag and conflict id are present (the `video` step). The claim text says "the machine implements the profile's windows" and never "the windows are N cycles".

**V6-4 [REC] Frame literals: format, derivation, and the independence guards.** `data/video/frames/<id>.frame` is ASCII with LF endings: comment header keys (`tier: source-expectation`, `oracle: independent-derivation`, `inputs:` the BACKTAB words and card patterns by id, `mode:`, `geometry:` literal id, derivation note), then one line per pixel row (96 rows), each line 159 hex digits (index 0 to F) with one space after every 8th digit so card boundaries are visible and the 20th card is 7 digits. If D8 keeps a 160-column nominal grid with a not-displayed marker, the marker is `.`, a row has 160 symbols and the geometry literal says which. Derivations live in `docs/video/frame-derivations.md`, per frame: BACKTAB words in hex and in binary with the fields marked, the card patterns line by line, the resulting 8 x 8 blocks, and the page or rule note for each field. Gate guards in the `video` step (pure text, no renderer): grammar and dimensions against `geometry.literal.txt`; symbol set; header keys and tier tag present; each frame is registered in `data/expected/video-frames.ids`; **per-card pixel-count conservation** (for each 8 x 8 block, count of pixels whose index equals the card's foreground index equals the popcount of the card's pattern bytes; legal because the fixtures use distinct foreground and background indices per card and a card never overlaps another); **hash tie** (`cmake -E sha256sum` of the frame's canonical ASCII form, a file the step writes with the spaces removed, equals the digest written in the `.expect` assert of the scenario and in the C test's generated include). The C tests compare the core's frame with a generated include of the literal byte by byte (and print the first mismatching card as two 8 x 8 hex blocks, a DX aid) and the runner compares a hash of the canonical ASCII form of the frame it received (a test-side helper in the runner library, not in `ivs_core`). Why ASCII for the tie: cmake cannot write a zero byte, so a binary canonical form cannot be produced by the independent tool.

**V6-5 [REC] The frame set (about 8) and what each isolates.** Each frame uses GRAM cards the fixture defines (four patterns authored for this project, geometric: stripes, box, diagonal, checker; no glyph shaped like a GROM character) on a background of zero-GROM cards.
| Frame | Mode | Content | Isolates |
|---|---|---|---|
| F-A1 | FG/BG | all cards blank, one background colour | bg index, full-frame size, 159 columns, all 12 rows |

**V6-6 [REC] Property and metamorphic tests (non-circular), with named ids.**
- `video.frame.count-equals-events`: over seeded runs the frames completed equals the number of timeline events inside the run's machine-time interval; a fault `STIC_FRAME_BY_MODULO` (frame derived as `now / F`) fails it at a start tick that is not frame-aligned.
- `video.slice.*` (frame bytes, video chain, `frames_completed`, `video_first_index`): one call, fixed 7, prime, seeded, one-unit slicing, with a stand-in guest that changes BACKTAB mid-run so a lazily rendered frame would differ if rendering depended on call boundaries.
- `video.boundary.event-minus-1`, `.event`, `.event-plus-1`: a frame event at tick `E` is included in the run that ends at a unit start `>= E` and not before; observed by single-stepping, as the input boundary tests did (the Phase 5 style: starts observed, not predicted from the cycle table).
- `video.meta.translate`, `video.meta.mode-agree`, `video.meta.permute`: the three metamorphic relations of section 2.
- `video.noop.null-buffer`: a NULL buffer with capacity 0 runs the same code against a counting sink and leaves the instance bytes identical to a run with a buffer (fault `VIDEO_STATE_DEPENDS_ON_BUFFER`, copied from the audio path).
- `video.buffers.sentinel`: a buffer pre-filled with a sentinel keeps its tail untouched; a refusal at capacity minus one writes nothing; `VIDEO_OUTBUF_PARTIAL_WRITE`.
- `video.chain.framing`: the video chain digest equals a hand-framed byte string hashed with `cmake -E sha256sum`; the runner recomputes the chain from the bytes it received (`video.chain.check`, as `audio.chain.check`).
- `stic.tile.units-equal-elapsed`: the sum of unit elapsed cycles equals the elapsed cycles exactly across grant, hold, release, interrupt-entry and instruction units.
- `stic.steal.accounting`: with display enabled over N whole frames, cycles in grant, hold and release units equal the stolen total the timeline reports; instructions retired by a constant-cost loop equal floor((total - stolen) / cost).

**V6-7 [REC] How SC1 and SC2 are proven when the numbers are policies.** (a) Access windows: a stand-in guest (hand-encoded words in test code, at most 24 words, each with a derivation comment, the Phase 5 `machine_standin` pattern) accesses a STIC address at observed start ticks around each window edge; the expected acceptance is `inside` or `outside` the edge, where the edge is read through the descriptor seam from the profile field being judged, and a second pass with the field moved by one cycle moves the observed boundary by exactly that cycle (`stic.window.edge-open-minus-1`, `.edge-open`, `.edge-close-1`, `.edge-close`, `stic.profile.window-open`, `.window-close`). The test proves the machine follows the profile; the number's accuracy is C1 and C2, `unknown-default-chosen`, D10. (b) Blocked policy rows: one row per class (STIC register read, STIC register write, GRAM read, GRAM write, GROM read), each tagged `policy` with the conflict id and the value or effect stated in a literal row (`unknown-default-chosen`: read gives `memmap.undriven_read`, write dropped, C5, C60), listed as policy not evidence. (c) Handshake: a sequence table (`stic.enable.seq.*`, about 10 rows): write enable in a window then the frame at the next boundary is produced; write outside the window is dropped and no frame is produced; a second write; the sustain rule (does the display stay enabled without a per-frame rewrite, C6, default taken from the wiki reading and labelled); reset clears it. (d) Interrupt and steal effect on CPU progress: a stand-in guest with `EIS` and a counting ISR gets one interrupt per frame (the count equals the number of frame events, a policy-free closed form), the interrupt is accepted at an interruptible boundary only, `DIS` blocks it, `INTRM` stays pending until `EIS` (CPU rules already proven in Phase 2; here the machine only drives the lines), and the accounting identity of V6-6 gives the effect on CPU progress. All the Phase 2 CPU claims stay with the Phase 2 vectors; Phase 6 does not retest them.

**V6-8 [REC] VID-06 inspection: byte equality plus a positive control.** The inspect path (peek through `ivs_machine_peek` for the register view, plus whatever state call the API plan adds for STIC mode, enable, stack pointer, frame counter, bus-steal state) is judged by: `memcmp` of the full instance storage and equality of every digest domain before and after each inspect call over all 128 STIC-decoded addresses, all aliases and `$3000-$3FFF`, in each of {display off, display on inside the window, display on outside the window, Color Stack mode, FG/BG mode}; the same sweep through the CPU path must show that a read of `$0021` in the window DOES switch the mode and a read outside it does not (`stic.peek.cpu-read-switches` is the positive control that stops the inspect sweep from passing vacuously); a read of `$0021` through inspect in the window leaves the mode unchanged (the roadmap's example); `ivs_machine_peek` shows "what a CPU read at that address would return now" including a closed gate (existing contract), with a note that a read whose CPU result would be a side effect has no side effect through peek (`stic.peek.value-equals-cpu-value` compares the value, not the side effect). Faults: `STIC_MODE_SWITCH_ON_PEEK` (the inspect path reuses the CPU read function) fails the sweep; `STIC_PEEK_ADVANCES_TIME` already exists as the Phase 4 peek fault and its must-fail ids extend.

### C. The guest diagnostic, D4, and the stub

**V6-9 [REC] `ivs-diag-stic-1`: shape, scenarios, mutants, consumer.** A third project diagnostic (kind `cart`, `fixtures/FX-diag-stic-1/diag-stic-1.listing.txt` plus `manifest.json`, MIT, redistributable yes, author-attested, one IVSC v1 segment at `5000`, entered through boot-stub **contract 1 unchanged**, no interrupt, no EXEC call, no GROM read). Program outline (all derived by hand in `docs/video/diag-stic-1-derivation.md` before the listing exists; about 130 words, limit 170, at most 6 branches):
1. Read `$01FE`; if the low byte is not `$FF` (a key is closed) take the Color Stack path, else the FG/BG path (one branch). This lets the scenario select the mode with an input event at tick 0 and avoids two image variants; it also exercises the Phase 5 read path in a Phase 6 end-to-end run, foreshadowing SCN-01.
2. Copy 4 card patterns (32 bytes) from a ROM data table to GRAM with `MVI@ R4` and `MVO@ R5` (only R4 and R5 auto-increment; the Phase 5 derivation already corrected that) in a counted loop.
3. Fill the 240 BACKTAB words `$0200-$02EF` with the blank card word through `MVO@ R4` in a counted loop; then 8 to 12 explicit writes of 14-bit card words through `SDBD; MVII` pairs (4 words) and `MVO` (2 words each).
4. Select the mode (the `$0021` read only on the Color Stack path), write the display-enable strobe (`$0020`, C6), then `HLT` and three `TSTR R0` followers.

## 6. Adversarial pass against this recommendation

1. **"Hand-derived frames from tier-4 rules are the card rules copied twice."** True for the field semantics: the independent parts are the geometry (tier 1), the popcount conservation, the second blind derivation, the metamorphic relations and the card-position structure. The record says `source-expectation, second-read`, tier-4 LOW, `provisional`; D10 or a tier 1 to 3 document is the only upgrade. The recommendation never claims the rules are right, only that the core reproduces the documented rules and that two readers agree on the frames.
2. **"If plan 06-01 finds the registers ARE printed (say, in the image of 5-12), the tier ladder changes."** Then those rules become tier-1 vectors and the claim ceiling moves; the plan structure does not change (V6-1 decision tree). It is the cheaper failure.
3. **"The page-reading prior is from a text layer; the figure may carry the numbers."** Agreed; that is why `timing-figure-readable` is `unknown` and 06-01 reads the image first. A figure that prints relative timings upgrades the structure layer (SR1 to SR2 spacing, lines per row) and creates new tier-1 literals.
4. **"Policy rows prove nothing."** They prove the machine implements the stated policy and that the policy is data; the claim wording says so. For the numbers C1 to C4 the evidence is a recorded absence and a D10 item, which is what the Core Value asks for.
5. **"Phase 5 overshot its estimate by 2.6 times; why trust 28 faults and 350 ids?"** The figures here are caps with stop rules, the costed items are the ones the notes measured (fault, control class, spawn), and the realised factor is shown beside the bottom-up number. The ids are not the cost. The faults are, and V6-12 puts a conversion path (judges) and a checkpoint before they are spent.
6. **"Doing a lever pass first delays the oracle work."** 06-02 is file-disjoint from 06-01, small, changes no evidence and is the pre-committed first lever. Skipping it leaves both triggers firing with no recorded action while 28 faults land.
7. **"The ratio `cpu_s / build_s` is invented."** It is computed from recorded rows, it is stable across a 2x speed range (6.4 to 7.4), and it separates code growth from runner speed, which is what the owner-set triggers cannot. It is read beside, not instead of, the raw triggers and is flagged imperfect (build time grows with code). The orchestrator may reject it; the plan does not depend on it except for the stop rule, which then falls back to the raw triggers.
8. **"Keeping the stub at contract 1 means the display cannot be sustained, so the diagnostic shows one frame."** Correct and sufficient for SC4. Sustain, interrupts per frame and `EIS` interplay are proven in-process by stand-ins. A project ISR is a Phase 7 need.
9. **"Selecting the mode by controller input couples the STIC diagnostic to Phase 5."** Yes, deliberately, and cheaply: it uses the one path both phases already test, and it avoids a second image. If a Phase 5 change breaks it, the failure names the clause.
10. **"A display-off STIC being timing-neutral is an assumption, not a test."** It is a falsifiable expectation with a named fault and a gate-level identity (older goldens byte-identical). If it fails, the re-bless classification must explain each moved line; the assumption is flagged `[UNK]` in section 9.
11. **"Eight 15 KB frames in the repository is heavy."** About 130 KB of text, reviewable line by line, no generator, no binary. The alternative (differences from a background only) loses the "no unintended pixels" property.
12. **"Every plan on the container proxy is 10 times 150 s."** About 25 minutes of unattended compute for a class of failure (gcc-only, glibc, `nm`) that has escaped more than once; Phase 5 ran it on several plans.

## 9. What this note assumes from the other Phase 6 decision points

- **STIC register and access architecture:** a register file at `$0000-$003F` with aliases, an access-window model driven by profile fields, a blocked policy per class, a display-enable strobe with a stated sustain rule, `$0021` read switching the card mode inside the window, an inspection call or peek semantics that never trigger the side effect, state inside the instance and the composite.
- **Frame and bus-steal timing model:** frames are timeline events at fixed ticks (not a modulo), a VBLANK interrupt line and BUSRQ steals expressed as one-cycle hold units plus a release unit through the existing stepper lines, 13 or 14 requests per frame when enabled and none when disabled, display-off is timing-neutral, the frame event time is defined in the profile.
- **Raw framebuffer and API:** a published geometry (D8) with byte layout row-major top-left 1 byte per pixel palette index 0 to 15 (and optionally a marker), `video_format` and `video_bytes_per_frame` in `ivs_info`, width and height published, the NULL-plus-0 rule and a slack for the overshoot, domain 16 chained by frame count independent of calls, `video_first_index` or an equivalent in the report; the request keeps `video` and `video_capacity`; appended fields carry pinned v1 sizes.
- **Runner and IVSS:** `capture video`, `assert video <k> sha256-text == <hex>` and a pixel form, `video.chain.check`, an operator `--frame-out`; no directive names a file.
- **Profile:** `stic.*` and `video.*` fields with tags and conflict ids; the geometry literal is a separate file the profile never feeds.
- **D4 and the stub:** contract 1 stays through Phase 6.
