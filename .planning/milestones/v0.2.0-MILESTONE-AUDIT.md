## Summary

The milestone delivered what it promised on the evidence axis and delivered less than it scoped on the
capability axis, and it did so honestly: eight of ten requirements are delivered, six of them cleanly
and four on terms that were written down before or at the moment they were needed; the two it did not
deliver (FMT-01, FMT-04) were dropped at a written entry gate with a reversal condition each, under a
fallback the milestone pre-authorized, and nothing was quietly lost. The gate did not grow (38 steps
before and after, 20 plans, zero new gate steps), no third-party byte entered the tree, `binary-ext.txt`
and `.gitignore` are untouched, no claim was minted or raised (28 claims at the start, 28 now), and the
registry phase 9 made derived survived phases 10 and 11 and closed its identity in both directions at
every phase boundary (`54−10=44`, `57−12=45`, `58−12=46`; at HEAD the step itself prints
`gates=110 lines=337`, exit 0). What is wrong is at the record and close-state layer, not the
capability layer: a phase whose verification body says `gaps_found` (4/5) carries `status: passed` in
its frontmatter and was closed by an orchestrator-appended section rather than a verifier re-run; a
hosted cost row recorded for phase 10 never reached `main` because it was committed on the phase branch
after the merge; the cost rule fired on the arithmetic and was interpreted away while the same number
was used as one ground to drop Phase 12; the close-state reconciliation has not run, so `CHANGELOG.md`
still says nothing is unreleased, the version macros still read 0.1.0, and STATE's Current Position
still says "Phase 12 — ready to plan". The three known imperfections are worse than their record
suggests in one respect (the false sentence is in the operator-facing specification, not an archive) and
better in another (the `r` reading is genuinely untrustworthy, and the arithmetic that proves it was
never printed). The milestone is archivable after a close-out commit that fixes records and one
one-line merge; it does not need a phase re-executed.

## Cost outcome

**Opening position (frozen):** corrected last-five median `r` **7.826**, budget 7.8, stop line 8.3, an
allowance of about **21 hosted cpu-s** (≈12.9 local at the recorded 1.675 factor) — "a ceiling, not a
target".

**Where it ended.** Recomputed from `.planning/ci-runs.tsv` on `main` in this session:

```
last five r: 10.4412  10.5882  10.7059  11.3514  11.6735   →  median 10.7059
```

against a stop line of 8.3. So the arithmetic is **2.41 above the line**, and the record's own claim is
that the number cannot be trusted at this spread. **Is that honest, or an excuse?** Honest — and the
record does not print the arithmetic that proves it. The end-of-milestone rows and the opening window:

| Window | rows | `build_s` | `cpu_s` | `r` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening (the five that settled Phase 9) | `bd6f2e3…93e6b9f` | 42–48 | 339–356 | 7.42–8.38 |
| End (the last two rows on `main`) | `cf2ffbc`, `ed523a4` | **34, 34** | **360, 364** | 10.59, 10.71 |

`cpu_s` is **back to the pre-milestone baseline** (360/364 against 339–356), while `build_s` fell from
42–48 to 34. The ratio rose ~25% because the **denominator** improved, not because the gate grew. The
milestone added, by the record's own unit costs, roughly 2.7 local cpu-s (wave A2 ≈1.2, four controls
≈0.28, two faults and their ids ≈1.0–2.0) against wave A1's −0.80, i.e. about **4.5 hosted cpu-s of the
21 allowed — roughly a fifth of the allowance**. That figure is this audit's arithmetic from the
recorded unit costs, not the record's own: **no phase 11 or close cost entry exists to price it**, which
is G-03.

## Evidence quality

Three known imperfections, judged rather than repeated.

**1. A false sentence left in the frozen oracle, correction appended 130 lines below.**
`docs/formats/ivsl-v1.md` tells an operator that "no 32-character hex sequence … is expressible anywhere
in the file (D-15)". It is false, it was measured false before the phase closed, and it is still there,
because GUARDRAILS 2.10 makes the oracle append-only. The override mechanism worked exactly as designed —
criterion 1 was not silently passed, the deviation is in the frontmatter with its reason, the amendment
states the measurement, and it admits the strongest thing a record can admit: **the conclusion survives
only because the premise it rested on failed** ("the value in that field is the operator's own scenario
id … never anything the images produced"). What this means for evidence quality is not that the
verification was dishonest; it is that the project applied a rule written for **archives and evidence
records** to a **live specification an operator reads**, and in that application the rule inverted its
own purpose: the artifact shipped with a known false statement in its most load-bearing paragraph, and a
reader who stops at the field table learns a guarantee the project knows to be untrue. A correction that
requires reading to line 218 of a 259-line document is not a correction for the person the document
exists for. It also means the milestone's own primary deliverable is not internally consistent, so any
future reader — or any future agent quoting the field table — inherits a false premise unless it reads
to the end. Severity low only because the property is not security- or rights-bearing in fact; the
evidence-quality cost is real.

**2. The `r` metric reads 10.7 against a stop line of 8.3, and the record argues it cannot be trusted.**
The argument is honest, and my own arithmetic makes it stronger than the record does: `cpu_s` at the end
of the milestone is **360/364**, statistically the same as the 339–356 of the window that settled Phase 9,
while `build_s` fell from 42–48 to **34**. The ratio rose because the denominator shrank. The gate did
not grow; the hosted builder got faster. So the record's conclusion is correct and its refusal to invent
a lever to chase a denominator is correct. But three things keep this from being clean: the decisive
arithmetic is in no record; the same number is used as authoritative in the Phase 12 entry gate and as
untrustworthy in `CI-EFFICIENCY.md`; and the milestone therefore closes with **no trustworthy cost signal
at all** — for the third consecutive milestone the headline cost metric is one the project says it cannot
read, and the replacement signal it names (`cpu_s` and the measured per-step profile) was recorded once,
in the phase 10 entry, and never carried into the phase 11 or close decisions. That is a genuine
methodological gap, not an excuse: the project has a rule that fires on a number it distrusts, and it has
neither replaced the number nor amended the rule.

**3. Three executors stopped by a 1800 s dispatch limit with plans closed out by the orchestrator, and
four finding rounds in work already verified `passed`.**
The tree holds three `*-REVIEW.md` records (09: 0 blockers / 3 warnings / 6 infos; 10: 2 blockers /
6 warnings / 2 infos; 11: 1 blocker / 4 warnings / 4 infos) plus a fourth finding round the gate itself
produced (`d3ff4bd`, after the Phase 9 closing records commit, fixing a proof line that a passing run
advertised as a failure and two requirement flips committed without their exclusion rows). Every one of
those findings landed in work a verifier had already marked `passed`. Two of the blockers were in the
phase's **central deliverable** (a handoff ledger that could be deleted, emptied or made unreadable with
the gate still green; a release rehearsal that exited 1 in every mode because its own new guard was
mis-wired). And three Phase 9 plans were closed out by the orchestrator — the same party that then
appended the hosted cost row, wrote the SUMMARY, and, in Phase 10, closed G-01 by appending a section to
the verifier's record. (The phase records disagree on the count: 09-04 says three executors, 09-06 says
five.) Three consequences, stated without softening:

- **`passed` in this project means "the verifier's own probes passed", not "the evidence is complete".**
  The reviews are load-bearing, not advisory. A reader who counts "3 of 3 phases passed" as assurance is
  over-reading it by exactly the defect class the reviews caught.
- **The verification's independence was preserved for the criteria and lost for the close-out.** Every
  frozen success criterion was judged by a verifier's own re-run, which is the right design. But the
  release-time proof (PKG-05's rehearsal) was closed by the orchestrator that broke it, and the phase's
  record now carries a status its own body contradicts. Independence that stops at the last inch is the
  inch that matters, because it is the inch the archive keeps.
- **Authorship of a commit is evidence, and it was recorded honestly.** `09-02-SUMMARY.md` states in
  terms that the orchestrator committed the executor's remaining work and wrote the summary, "recorded
  because authorship of a commit is evidence, not bookkeeping". That is the right instinct; what is
  missing is the same instinct applied to the close-out of G-01, where the record does not distinguish
  a verifier's re-measurement from the orchestrator's own probe. Fixing G-01 with a re-verification, or
  with a frontmatter that says which it was, is the whole of the remedy.

## The CI-row termination rule (found by walking into it, 2026-11-25)

Recording a hosted run needs a commit; a commit to `main` needs a PR; a PR merge is itself a hosted
push run. **Every row therefore generates the next row.** This close walked four links into that
regress before naming it, which is exactly the kind of unbounded process this project's rules exist
to catch.

**The rule, so it is not rediscovered:**

> The rows for a milestone's close are recorded in **one** records PR. **The push run created by that
> PR's own merge is the terminal run of the milestone: it is recorded as the first row of the next
> milestone's opening commit**, never in a follow-up PR of its own.

The terminal run is named in the milestone audit so nothing is lost: for v0.2.0 it is the merge push
of the records PR that carries this note, head `1ee95ea`'s successor. Its row lands with v0.3.0's
opening commit, which is a commit the next milestone makes anyway.

**Why not just leave it unrecorded:** an unrecorded hosted run is invisible, and this milestone spent
a phase proving that invisible evidence rots. The row is deferred by exactly one commit and its id is
named here, so it is deferred rather than dropped. **Why not keep going:** the fifth link would need
a sixth, and a rule that cannot terminate is not a rule.
